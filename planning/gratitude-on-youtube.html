Gratitude on YouTube

Who is actually talking about gratitude, how big they are, and which videos are worth reacting to. The headline: “gratitude” on YouTube is three different audiences wearing the same word, and only one of them is yours.

Three audiences, one keyword

Search “gratitude” and the results are overwhelmingly not what you’d assume. Knowing which lane you’re entering matters more than any single video choice.

  • Prayer and worship Skip By far the biggest slice. Morning prayer videos, worship instrumentals, Brandon Lake’s song Gratitude in a dozen lyric-video re-uploads. You cannot react to a prayer, and trying reads as disrespectful.
  • Manifestation and affirmations Avoid “108 Thank Yous,” abundance affirmations, 777 portals, law of attraction. High volume, and the heaviest overclaiming anywhere in the topic. Easy to dunk on, but it drags in an audience that wants manifestation, not fatherhood.
  • Ideas and evidence Your lane Kurzgesagt, TED and TEDx, Jim Kwik, Psych2Go. Much smaller, but it is the only lane where anyone makes an argument — which is the only thing you can meaningfully react to. It is also the lane where your research grounding is a genuine edge.

The gap

Nobody is making “gratitude practice, for dads.”

I searched gratitude crossed with fatherhood across all time. What came back: Father’s Day tribute songs, a local news clip, a monkey birthday video and a Nollywood film. The intersection is empty.

Everything below is a video about gratitude in general. Not one is about gratitude for a man who is failing at bedtime and knows it. That is the whole reason a reaction from you is worth watching rather than just another summary.

Worth reacting to

Start here

How gratitude can fuel your success

Jeremy Stoker · TEDx Swansea · July 2025

1.0M views 38.2K likes 3.8% like rate 103 views/hr still outlier 361

An airline captain. A father of three. He hit rock bottom and used gratitude to get back to the left-hand seat. A million views in seven months and it is still pulling a hundred views an hour, which means the algorithm has not finished with it.

Your angle

You run a Captain’s Log. He is a literal captain who says gratitude saved his career. That coincidence is the video. Agree with him on the practice, then add the thing his talk doesn’t have: the actual numbers, and a log built so a man can miss a month without quitting.

The big one

An Antidote to Dissatisfaction

Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell · 25.5M subscribers

16.9M views 817K likes 4.8% like rate 25.5K comments

The definitive gratitude video on the internet, by an order of magnitude. And here is the part that matters: Kurzgesagt sells a gratitude journal too. They built the same product you did, for a general audience, off the same literature.

Your angle

Not a takedown. A practitioner’s footnote: beautiful animation is easy to admire and hard to actually do. What happens when a real dad runs 28 entries? Where does it break? Their journal has no answer for the night you miss. Yours does.

Pair with the above

We Could All Use a Little Change

vlogbrothers · John Green · 4.09M subscribers

311K views 22.0K likes 7.1% like rate 4 min

John Green on how Kurzgesagt’s gratitude journal changed his life. The highest like rate in this entire set, and it is four minutes long. A famous author, publicly open about his mental health, saying a journal helped.

Your angle

React to Kurzgesagt and this back to back as one video: the argument, then a real man who tried it. Green is the proof of concept for your entire Day 0.

Where your research pays

How Gratitude Rewires Your Brain

Jim Kwik · 1.94M subscribers · 2022

495K views 3.1% engagement 11 min

“Rewires your brain” is the single most common claim in this topic, and it runs well ahead of the evidence. You now know what the meta-analyses actually say.

Your angle

Be generous, not combative. “He’s mostly right and the language is oversold” is a better video than a dunk. Then give the real figure — roughly g = 0.19 to 0.22 — and make the case that a small effect at ninety seconds a night is still the best deal in self-improvement.

The contrarian

I Have a Gratitude Problem

Mal Wrenn Corbin · TEDx Boston College · recent

75K views 18% engagement outlier 193

Small by views, but the highest engagement rate of anything I found by a wide margin. People argue about this one. A talk that is sceptical of gratitude is far more interesting to react to than another one praising it.

Your angle

You get to be the balanced one. You already hold both halves: gratitude is real and small, it is not a treatment, and it will not fix the thing the man is actually frightened of.

The serious one

How gratitude rewires your brain

Christina Costa · TED and TEDx UofM · ~524K combined

311K TEDx 213K TED 3.2% like rate

A psychology PhD researching wellbeing who was diagnosed with a brain tumour mid-study, then used her own research on herself. She is the closest thing here to your own evidence-first stance.

Your angle

No pushback needed. Use it to make the providing under pressure point: gratitude was studied on people living with serious illness, not just comfortable undergraduates. This practice was built for hard conditions.

Only if you want the fight

The Healing Power of Gratitude — Telomeres

Bruce Lipton, PhD · 627K subscribers

544K views 3.4% like rate

Claims gratitude engages telomerase and slows ageing. This is the furthest overreach in the topic, and correcting it is genuinely useful.

Your angle

Read the room first. His audience is devoted and the comments get unpleasant. It is a different kind of video from everything above, and it will bring a different kind of viewer. Worth doing eventually. Not worth doing first.

How to play it

  • Bring the dad, not the summary. Every one of these videos already explains gratitude perfectly well. Your only defensible edge is the one nobody else has: a man who runs a community of fathers, holding a log he built, with eleven real replies about failure and short fuses and no money.
  • Lead with agreement. Reaction videos that open by trashing someone get one kind of audience. Yours should open by taking the person seriously, then adding the thing they left out. That is also just closer to how you write.
  • The correction is your format. “Rewires your brain” appears everywhere. You can now say, calmly and with sources, what is actually known. Nobody else in this niche is doing that, and it is the most repeatable segment you have.
  • Every video ends in the same place. Three things you’re grateful for, an Echo, and no streak to break. The reaction is the hook; the log is the offer.

About this data

Pulled from vidIQ on 2 September 2026: outlier scans on gratitude, gratitude science, and gratitude crossed with fatherhood, plus a view-ranked search inside YouTube’s knowledge topic. Like rate is likes divided by views. Outlier and breakout scores measure a video against its own channel’s norm, so a high score on a small channel is not the same as a high score on TED.

View counts move. Re-run before you script anything, and check each video is still up.
