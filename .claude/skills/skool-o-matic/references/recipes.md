# Starter recipes — the tools operators ask for most

Each recipe names the wish behind it, the shape it takes, and how to run it. Start from a recipe whenever the interview lands near one — a tuned classic beats a from-scratch build. Every recipe obeys guardrails.md (read-only gathering; drafts handed over, never sent).

## 1. Quiet Member Sweep (the most-wanted tool on Skool)

**The wish:** "I feel like people are slipping away and I don't notice until they're gone."
**Shape:** routine → mini-skill.

Walk the members page. Flag members whose activity hint shows quiet for longer than the operator's threshold (default: 14 days — ask). Split the flags into two very different lists:

- **New & quiet** — joined in the last ~30 days, Level 1, gone quiet. These people never landed. The move is a low-pressure "how's it going, what were you hoping to find here?" note.
- **Faded regulars** — Level 3+, previously visible on leaderboards or in the feed, now quiet. These are the valuable saves. The move is personal and specific ("your post about X is still getting replies").

Deliver at most ~8 names per run, highest-value first, each with the one-line why and a draft note in the operator's voice. More than 8 becomes homework nobody does.

## 2. Welcome Sweep

**The wish:** "I want every new member to get a personal hello, but I can't keep up."
**Shape:** routine → mini-skill.

Find members who joined since the last sweep (ask how far back on the first run). For each: pull the human details from their profile — bio line, joining-question answers if visible, anything specific. Draft a two-sentence personal welcome per person referencing a real detail, in the operator's voice. Hand over the list; they send. For private communities, this pairs with the membership-requests page: summarize each pending request's answers and draft the welcome for approval-day.

## 3. Celebration Radar

**The wish:** "I want to catch wins and shout people out, but I miss them."
**Shape:** routine, often merged into the Quiet Member Sweep's mini-skill as a second section.

Check the 7-day and 30-day leaderboards and the level-up progress area. Surface: new faces in the top 10, anyone about to level up, and a first-post from a previously silent member (scan recent feed). Draft one public shout-out post or per-person congrats comments. Public celebration is the highest-leverage 5 minutes an operator has — say so.

## 4. Community Pulse Report

**The wish:** "How is my community actually doing?" / "I want a dashboard."
**Shape:** report. For communities over ~150 members, prefer the member CSV export as the data source.

One page, five numbers with a plain-English sentence each: members (and 30-day change if knowable), share of feed posts in the last 7 days vs the prior 7, top 3 liveliest categories, leaderboard turnover (new names in top 10 this month), and the quiet count from recipe 1. Then ONE recommended action for the week — a report that ends in an action gets reread; a wall of charts doesn't. If they want it pretty, build a simple single-file HTML page they can reopen.

## 5. Post Doctor

**The wish:** "What should I post? Why did that post die?"
**Shape:** writing assist.

Read their last ~20 feed posts: which got comments (not just likes), which categories, what kind of opening line. Report the pattern in two sentences. Then draft 3 post options for this week that fit what THEIR community demonstrably responds to — grounded in named evidence ("your question-style posts average 4x the comments of your announcement-style posts").

## 6. Event Follow-Through

**The wish:** "People RSVP and don't show / I never follow up after events."
**Shape:** routine around the calendar.

Before an event: pull the RSVP list, draft a personal day-before nudge per person (operator sends). After: draft a recap post and thank-you notes referencing what happened. The follow-up half is where communities differentiate — most operators skip it.

## Combining recipes

The natural end-state for an engaged operator is one **Morning Coffee mini-skill**: quiet flags + new-member welcomes + celebrations in a single 10-minute pass. Offer the combination only after they've run two recipes separately and liked them — bundling too early makes the first run overwhelming.
